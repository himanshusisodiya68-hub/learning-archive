Refraction and Snell's law

Appears in Class 10 Science · Light  ·  Class 12 Physics · Ray Optics Levels Class 10 → Olympiad practised
Needs Laws of reflection Speed of light Sine of an angle
Try first
A coin lies at the bottom of a bucket filled with water to a depth of 40 cm. Looking straight down, how deep does the coin seem to be?

The idea

Light travels at about 3 × 108 m/s in vacuum and more slowly inside matter. When a beam crosses at an angle from one material into another, one edge of the beam slows down before the other. The beam swings round, the way a column of marchers turns when one end steps into mud first.

The refractive index of a medium compares the two speeds: n = c/v. Water has n ≈ 1.33 and ordinary glass about 1.5. A larger n means slower light and a sharper bend towards the normal.

n1sinθ1=n2sinθ2 Both angles are measured from the normal, the line perpendicular to the surface.

See it move

Worked example

A ray in air strikes a glass slab (n = 1.5) at 60° to the normal. Find the angle of refraction.
  1. Write the law: 1.00 × sin 60° = 1.5 × sin θ2.
  2. sin θ2 = 0.866 / 1.5 = 0.577.
  3. θ2 = sin−1(0.577) ≈ 35.3°. The ray bends towards the normal, as expected for a slower medium.

Boardsample problem✓ answer checked by code

Light enters water (n = 1.33) from air at an angle of incidence of 45°. Find the angle of refraction, in degrees.

Hint 1

Only the medium you are entering changes. Air has n = 1.00.

Hint 2

sin θ2 = sin 45° / 1.33.

Solution

sin θ2 = 0.7071 / 1.33 = 0.5317, so θ2 ≈ 32.1°.

JEE Mainsample problem✓ answer checked by code

A layer of oil 6 cm thick (n = 1.5) floats on water 8 cm deep (n = 4/3). Seen from directly above, how far below the oil surface does the bottom of the vessel appear?

Hint

For near-normal viewing, each layer appears shallower by a factor of its own refractive index, and the apparent depths add.

Solution

6/1.5 + 8/(4/3) = 4 + 6 = 10 cm.

Olympiadsample problemnot yet verified

Light goes from point A in medium 1 to point B in medium 2 along the path that takes the least time (Fermat's principle). Derive Snell's law from this.

Hint 1

Let the ray cross the boundary at a distance x along it. Write the total travel time T(x) using the speeds c/n1 and c/n2.

Hint 2

Set dT/dx = 0 and read each term as the sine of an angle.

In your own words

HistoryIbn Sahl described the law of refraction in Baghdad around 984. Willebrord Snell found it again in 1621, and it carries his name.
Board favouriteApparent depth = real depth ÷ n, valid only when viewing nearly straight down.
Olympiad linkSnell's law follows from least time. The same reasoning later gives the brachistochrone and Lagrangian mechanics.
