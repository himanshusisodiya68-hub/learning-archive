Your subjects
How these lessons teach: the Feynman way
Start with a real questionEvery lesson opens with something you have wondered about. The idea arrives as the answer, so you know why it exists before you see a formula.
Plain words first, notation lastSymbols are shorthand for sentences you already understand. If an idea cannot be said simply, it is not yet understood: Feynman's test.
Same idea, many placesGravity and electric force share one shape; a hash table and a library index share one trick. Seeing the common pattern turns facts into mental models you can use anywhere.
Each answer raises the next questionEvery lesson ends on a question the next one answers. Follow the chain and a whole branch of your knowledge tree lights up.
Recall, then space it outQuick checks pull ideas from memory, and reviews return just before you would forget. A few minutes a day is enough.
