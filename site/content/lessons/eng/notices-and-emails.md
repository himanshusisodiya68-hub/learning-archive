## What the reader expects {lvl=1 id=s-reader label="Foundation"}

Before any layout, ask who is reading and what they need. A reader of a notice or an email is usually busy, has other things to do and wants to leave knowing one thing: what must I do, and by when?

Write for that reader and the rules of the format follow. A **notice** goes to a crowd, so it must stand alone, be short and give every fact. An **email** goes to a person, so it must say who is asking, what for and when.

| | Notice | Formal email |
|---|---|---|
| **Reader** | Many people, often unknown | One person or one team, known by role |
| **Purpose** | Announce, warn, invite, appeal | Request, complain, apply, reply, inform |
| **Length** | About 50 words in most board papers [Unverified] | About 100 to 120 words in most board papers [Unverified] |
| **Opening** | The issuer and the word NOTICE | Salutation after a clear subject line |
| **Tone** | Impersonal, brief, polite | Polite, direct, first person (I, we) |
| **Closing** | Name and designation only | Closing phrase (Yours sincerely) and name |
| **Reply expected?** | Rarely; a contact person is named if needed | Yes; the ask and deadline are stated |

:::note title="Know the limit" tone=tip
Word limits in the question beat any number in this lesson. If your question says "in not more than 50 words", count only the body, not the heading, date or signature, unless your teacher says otherwise [Unverified]. Stay inside the limit; examiners can stop reading past it.
:::

## The notice layout {lvl=1 id=s-notice label="Foundation"}

Every notice answers five questions: **what** is happening, **when**, **where**, **who** it is for and **what to do**. The layout puts those answers in a fixed order so the reader's eye finds them quickly.

| Part | What goes here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Issuer** | Name of the school, society or office, in capitals, at the top | SUNRISE PUBLIC SCHOOL, PUNE |
| **Word NOTICE** | The word itself, centred | NOTICE |
| **Date** | The day it is issued, in full | 12 November 2026 |
| **Heading** | The subject in 3 to 6 words, often underlined | Inter-House Debate Competition |
| **Body** | Event, date and time, venue, who may attend, what to do, who to contact | See the model in the next section |
| **Writer** | Name and designation, at the bottom left or right | Ritu Sharma, Head Girl |

Three small rules decide most of the marks for form.

- A notice is addressed to no one, so there is **no Dear and no Yours sincerely**.
- Write the date and time in full: *Friday, 27 November 2026, at 10 a.m.*, not "next week".
- The writer signs with a name and a post (Head Girl, Secretary, Principal), because a notice is an official statement and the reader needs to know who stands behind it.

:::qc
q: Which of these does NOT belong in a school notice?
options: ["The heading", "The date", "Dear students,", "The name and designation of the writer"]
answer: 2
right: Right. A notice is meant for everyone and has no salutation. Dear belongs in letters and emails.
wrong: A notice has an issuer, the word NOTICE, a date, a heading, a body and a signature line. It is addressed to everyone, so it has no Dear.
:::

## The email layout {lvl=1 id=s-email-parts label="Foundation"}

An email is a message to one reader, and the reader sees the top before anything else. The parts come in this order.

| Part | What goes here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **To** | The receiver's address (and a Cc if others should know) | customercare@bookbazaar.example |
| **Subject** | Topic, and the action or date if there is one | Wrong book delivered, order no. 48213 |
| **Salutation** | Dear + title and name; Dear Sir/Madam if unknown | Dear Customer Care Team, |
| **Opening line** | Who you are or why you are writing, in one sentence | I ordered a book on 3 November 2026. |
| **Body** | The details, then the request, then the deadline | See the model later in this lesson |
| **Closing** | Yours sincerely (named reader) or Yours faithfully (Dear Sir/Madam) | Yours sincerely, |
| **Name** | Your full name, and a contact detail if useful | Aman Verma |

(In an exam you write the To and Subject lines yourself, as the examiner cannot see an inbox. Add From or Cc only if the question gives or asks for them.)

:::qc
q: You are writing to "The Principal" and do not know her name. Which pair is correct?
options: ["Dear Sir/Madam ... Yours faithfully", "Dear Sir/Madam ... Yours sincerely", "Hi Principal ... Thanks", "Dear Madam ... Love"]
answer: 0
right: Right. Without a name, the traditional pair is Dear Sir/Madam with Yours faithfully. Use Dear Ms Rao with Yours sincerely only when you know the name.
wrong: The standard match is Dear Sir/Madam with Yours faithfully for an unknown reader, and a named reader with Yours sincerely. Hi and Love are too casual for a formal message.
:::

## A model notice {lvl=2 id=s-model-notice label="Class 10"}

Here is a notice that a board examiner would mark well. The question: *Your school plans an Inter-House Debate. As the Head Girl, Ritu Sharma, write a notice in not more than 50 words.*

:::worked title="Model notice, 49 words in the body"
**SUNRISE PUBLIC SCHOOL, PUNE**

**NOTICE**

12 November 2026

**Inter-House Debate Competition**

The school will hold an Inter-House Debate on Friday, 27 November 2026, at 10 a.m. in the auditorium. Topic: 'Social media: a blessing or a curse?' Students of Classes IX to XII who wish to take part should give their names to Ms Rao, Room 12, by 20 November.

Ritu Sharma

Head Girl
:::

How the notice does its job:

1. The **heading** says what the notice is about before the body starts.
2. The first sentence packs in **what, when and where** (Friday, 27 November 2026, 10 a.m., the auditorium).
3. A short second sentence gives the **topic**, so students can decide whether to take part.
4. The last sentence gives **who** may join, **what to do**, **whom to see** and a **deadline**.
5. There is no greeting and no closing. The writer's name and post sit at the foot.

:::note title="Counting the words" tone=tip
The body above has 49 words. Count the words of the body only, with each date, number and abbreviation as one word. If you are over, cut adjectives first ("grand", "exciting", "much-awaited") and keep every fact.
:::

:::worked title="Spot the missing facts"
A student wrote: *Dear students, we are going to have a sports day soon, so please come and bring your sports kit. Principal.*

What is wrong? **No issuer, no word NOTICE, no date and no heading.** The body gives no date, time or venue, never says who may take part and gives no deadline or contact. It also opens with Dear, which a notice never does. Fixing it means supplying each missing fact, with sensible details if the question gives none, and removing the greeting.
:::

:::qc
q: Which sentence belongs in the body of a notice about a blood donation camp?
options: ["Come and donate blood because it is a noble act.", "A blood donation camp will be held in the school hall on Saturday, 5 December 2026, from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. Students and staff above 18 may donate.", "Dear all, we hope you will come to the camp.", "The camp is going to be very exciting and fun for all."]
answer: 1
right: Right. It gives what, when, where and who in plain words. The other options are vague, personal or promotional.
wrong: A good body states the facts a reader needs: what is happening, the date and time, the place, who may take part. The other options give no date, time or place.
:::

## A model formal email {lvl=2 id=s-model-email label="Class 10"}

The question: *You are Aman Verma. A wrong book has been delivered to you by an online bookshop. Write an email of about 100 words to the customer care manager.*

:::worked title="Model formal email, 87 words in the body"
**To:** customercare@bookbazaar.example

**Subject:** Wrong book delivered, order no. 48213

Dear Customer Care Team,

I ordered 'Wings of Fire' (order no. 48213) on 3 November 2026. The parcel that reached me on 9 November holds a different book, a Hindi grammar guide. I have kept the packaging and the book unused.

Please send me the book I ordered, or refund the price of Rs 250 to my account, and tell me how to return the wrong book. I would be grateful for a reply by 16 November 2026, as the book is a birthday gift for my brother on 20 November.

Yours sincerely,

Aman Verma
:::

Why it works, paragraph by paragraph.

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Subject line** | Names the problem and carries the order number, so the shop can find the record before opening the email |
| **Paragraph 1** | The facts: what was ordered, when, what arrived, and the state of the parcel |
| **Paragraph 2** | The **ask** (send the right book or refund), the steps wanted, a reason and a deadline |
| **Closing** | Yours sincerely, because the reader is addressed as a team and the writer wants a formal close |

Notice what is left out: no long apology, no anger, no repetition. A calm, specific email is taken more seriously than an angry one.

:::qc
q: Which line would be best placed in the second paragraph of a complaint email?
options: ["Your service is the worst I have ever seen.", "Please replace the book or refund Rs 250 by 16 November 2026.", "I am writing to you today because I wanted to tell you something.", "I hope you are doing well."]
answer: 1
right: Right. It states the ask and a date. A complaint works when the reader knows exactly what you want and when.
wrong: A complaint should be specific. Pick the line that says what you want and by when, not the one that insults or only fills space.
:::

## Marking checklist {lvl=2 id=s-checklist label="Class 10"}

Boards generally give marks for **format**, **content** and **expression** (accuracy of language and fluency) [Unverified: the exact split changes by year and board, so check the current marking scheme]. Use this table to mark your own answer before you move on.

| Check | Notice | Email |
|---|---|---|
| **Format** | Issuer, NOTICE, date, heading, body, name and designation; no Dear or closing | To, Subject, salutation, body in paragraphs, closing phrase, name |
| **Content** | All five of what, when, where, who and action; sensible invented details if none are given | Purpose in the first sentence; details; one clear ask; a deadline; a reason |
| **Length** | Within the stated limit (about 50 words) | Within the stated limit (about 100 to 120 words) |
| **Tone** | Impersonal, brief, polite | Formal, polite, firm if a complaint; no slang, no contractions |
| **Accuracy** | Correct tenses (future for events, present perfect for news), spelling, punctuation | Correct salutation and closing pair, correct tenses, spelling |
| **Last look** | Dates in full, names spelt the same throughout | Subject line matches the body; attachment mentioned only if there is one |

:::qc
q: A notice has a perfect layout but leaves out the venue and the time. Which category of marks does it mainly lose?
options: ["Format", "Content", "Expression", "None"]
answer: 1
right: Right. The layout is correct, but a missing venue and time are missing facts, and facts are content.
wrong: The layout (format) and the language are fine. What is missing are facts the reader needs, which is content.
:::

## Formal and informal register {lvl=2 id=s-register label="Class 10"}

**Register** is the level of formality in your language. A formal email goes to a principal, an officer, an editor or a company. An informal email goes to a friend or a relative. The same message can be written in either; the choice depends only on the reader.

| | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| **Salutation** | Dear Sir/Madam, Dear Ms Rao, | Hi Neha, Dear Neha, |
| **Opening** | I am writing to request ... | Hope you are well! I wanted to ask ... |
| **Request** | I would be grateful if you could ... | Could you please ... / Can you ... |
| **Contractions** | Avoided: do not, cannot | Allowed: don't, can't |
| **Closing** | Yours sincerely / Yours faithfully | Take care / See you soon / Love |
| **Words** | Request, inform, regret, assist | Ask, tell, sorry, help |

- Prefer **polite modals** in formal requests: *would, could, may*. "I want a refund" sounds harsh; "I would like a refund" does not.
- Use the **passive** and impersonal phrases in notices: *Students are requested to ...*, *Entries must be submitted by ...*.
- Use **linkers** (so, since, therefore, as) sparingly to show why: *As the book is a gift, I would be grateful for an early reply.*

:::qc
q: Which sentence is suitable for a formal email to a principal?
options: ["Can you let us go on the trip? It will be fun.", "I would be grateful if you could grant permission for the trip.", "We want to go on the trip, so please say yes.", "Hey, can we go?"]
answer: 1
right: Right. It uses the polite modal construction would be grateful if you could and no slang.
wrong: Formal requests use polite modals and full forms. The other options are too casual or too direct for a principal.
:::

## What the board asks {lvl=2 id=s-board label="Class 10"}

Questions on notices and emails come in a handful of set patterns. Note that the current CBSE Class 10 paper centres on a formal letter and an analytical paragraph for its writing items [Unverified: check the latest sample paper]. Notices and emails still appear in school-level exams, in other boards and in the Class 9 and 11 syllabus, and the skills carry over directly to a formal letter.

| Question type | What must be in it | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Event notice** (sports day, debate, fair) | What, date, time, venue, who may take part, how to register, contact | Invented details must be sensible |
| **Change or cancellation** | What changes, old and new date, reason in a clause, who is affected | Say what stays the same |
| **Lost and found** | Item, description, place and time lost or found, where to collect, reward if any | The contact person, not just "contact me" |
| **Appeal or warning** (blood camp, water saving) | The cause, the need, the action, the date | Keep an official tone, no lecture |
| **Complaint email** | Order or reference number, the fault, the remedy wanted, a deadline | Facts, not abuse |
| **Request or permission email** | Who you are, what you want, dates, why, thanks and deadline | One request only |
| **Reply email** | Answers every point of the mail you received | Keep the thread's subject line |

When the question gives only a skeleton, **invent the missing details** (date, time, a plausible name for the contact). Examiners expect it, so long as the details are sensible and consistent.

## Subject lines that get opened {lvl=3 id=s-subject label="Beyond the board"}

At work, people see only the sender and the subject line before they decide whether to open an email. A good subject line works like a **label on a file**: it says what is inside, so the reader can decide when to deal with it and can find it again in a month.

| Weak | Why it fails | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| Hello | Says nothing | Intern joining on Monday: desk and laptop needed |
| Meeting | Which meeting? | Project review moved to Thursday, 3 p.m., Room B |
| Question | About what? | Question: which date for the Q3 audit? |
| Urgent!!! | Everything is urgent, so nothing is | Approval needed by Friday, 5 p.m.: Q3 travel budget |
| FW: Re: Re: Report | Hides the point | FYI: final sales report attached, no action needed |
| (blank) | Looks like spam | Always write something |

Patterns that work:

- **Topic + action + date**: *Contract review: your sign-off needed by 25 Oct*.
- **A short tag in front**: *Action:*, *FYI:*, *Decision:*, *Question:*. The tag tells the reader how much attention to give before opening.
- **Keep it short**: put the main words first, since a phone shows only the first 40 or so characters [Inference: the exact limit varies by device].
- **Change the subject when the topic changes.** If a thread about a budget turns into a question about a trip, start a new email so the old subject does not mislead anyone.

:::qc
q: Which subject line is best for an email asking your manager to approve leave from 3 to 7 December?
options: ["Leave", "Please read", "Leave request: 3 to 7 December, approval needed by 20 Nov", "Hi"]
answer: 2
right: Right. It names the topic, the dates and the deadline, so your manager knows what it is before opening.
wrong: Pick the line that gives the topic, the dates and a deadline. The others say too little to act on.
:::

## One email, one ask {lvl=3 id=s-clarity label="Beyond the board"}

Clarity at work follows a simple order: **lead with the point, then give the reasons, then repeat what is needed.** Many offices call this putting the bottom line first [Inference: the term is common in business writing, not an official standard].

1. **First two lines:** why you are writing and what you need.
2. **Next lines:** only the background the reader needs to decide.
3. **Last line:** the ask and the date, once more, then thanks.

Other habits that make emails easy to answer:

- **One email, one topic.** If you must raise two, number them and make each a single question.
- **Short paragraphs** of two or three sentences, with a blank line between. A wall of text is skimmed.
- **Dates and times in full**, with the time zone for remote teams: *Thursday, 5 November, 4 p.m. IST*.
- **Say what is attached**, and attach it: *The signed form is attached (form.pdf).*
- **Avoid empty phrases.** *Please do the needful* and *kindly revert* make the reader guess. Say what: *Please send the signed form by Friday.* *Please reply by Friday.*

:::worked title="Before and after"
**Before.** Subject: Report. Hi, I hope you are fine. I was thinking about the sales report and there are some numbers I am not sure about, and also the deadline for it, and I wanted to ask if you could look at it sometime. Also, the printer is not working. Thanks.

**What is wrong.** The subject says nothing. The ask is buried. There are three topics (numbers, deadline, printer) and no date.

**After.**
Subject: Sales report: please check totals by Wednesday

Hi Priya,

Could you check the regional totals in the attached sales report by Wednesday, 4 p.m.? I am unsure about the East figure on page 2.

Two questions:
1. Is the final deadline still Friday?
2. Can we use the draft figures for the Thursday call?

Thanks, and I will raise the printer problem with IT separately.

Neel
:::

## Who gets a copy {lvl=3 id=s-copy label="Beyond the board"}

Every address field sends a signal about what the reader should do.

| Field | Who goes here | What it tells them |
|---|---|---|
| **To** | The people who must act or answer | You are expected to respond |
| **Cc** | People who should know but need not act | For information only |
| **Bcc** | A large group whose addresses should stay private, or a quiet record for yourself | Others cannot see them; use carefully |
| **Reply** | Only the sender | Use this by default |
| **Reply-all** | Everyone on the thread | Only when everyone needs your answer |

Good habits before you press send:

- **Check the recipient.** Autocomplete can fill in the wrong name; sending a private message to the wrong person is one of the commonest workplace errors.
- **Read it once as the reader.** Does the first line tell them what to do?
- **Keep it kind and calm.** An email can be forwarded and kept for years. Avoid writing when angry; wait an hour.
- **Do not put anything private** (passwords, bank details, someone else's secret) in an email unless a policy says it is safe.
- **Follow up politely** if there is no reply after the deadline: *Following up on my email of 5 November: could you confirm by Friday?*

:::qc
q: You want your team to see a decision but only your manager must act on it. How do you address the email?
options: ["Everyone in To", "Manager in To, team in Cc", "Manager in Bcc, team in To", "Everyone in Bcc"]
answer: 1
right: Right. To marks who must act and Cc marks who should simply know.
wrong: Put the person who must act in To and those who only need to know in Cc. Bcc hides people and does not suit this case.
:::

## Key ideas to remember {lvl=1 id=s-keys}

:::keys
1. Write for a busy reader: put the facts they need first and cut everything else. {lvl=1}
2. A notice answers what, when, where, who and what to do in about 50 words. {lvl=1}
3. Notice layout: issuer, NOTICE, date, heading, body, name and designation. No Dear and no closing line. {lvl=1}
4. Email layout: To, Subject, salutation, body, closing, name. The subject line names the topic and, if it applies, the action and date. {lvl=1}
5. Dear Sir/Madam goes with Yours faithfully; Dear + a name goes with Yours sincerely. {lvl=1}
6. A good body has a purpose, the details, one clear ask and a deadline. {lvl=2}
7. Use formal register for a principal, an officer or a company: polite modals, no contractions, no slang. {lvl=2}
8. Check your own work for format, content and expression, and stay inside the word limit. {lvl=2}
9. When a question gives too few facts, invent sensible, consistent details. {lvl=2}
10. At work, write subject lines that work as file labels, lead with the ask, send one email per topic, and use To, Cc and reply-all deliberately. {lvl=3}
:::

## Toolkit and traps {lvl=1 id=s-traps}

:::traps
- Leaving out the date, time or venue in a notice: the reader cannot act. {lvl=1}
- Starting a notice with "Dear students" or ending with "Yours faithfully": a notice has neither. {lvl=1}
- Forgetting the name and designation at the end of a notice. {lvl=1}
- A blank or vague subject line ("Request", "Hello") on an email. {lvl=1}
- Using Dear Sir/Madam with Yours sincerely, or a named reader with Yours faithfully. {lvl=2}
- Going over the word limit, or counting the heading and signature in the body count without checking the question. {lvl=2}
- Copying a notice's chatty or promotional tone ("a really fun day for all!") into an official announcement. {lvl=2}
- Contractions, slang or demands ("I want a refund now") in a formal email. {lvl=2}
- Asking three things in one email, so the reader answers one. {lvl=3}
- Replying to all when only the sender needs the answer, and sending to the wrong autocompleted address. {lvl=3}
- Filler like "please do the needful" or "kindly revert": say exactly what you want and by when. {lvl=3}
:::
