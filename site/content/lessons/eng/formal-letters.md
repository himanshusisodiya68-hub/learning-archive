## Why a letter has a fixed shape {lvl=1 id=s-why label="Foundation"}

When you write to a friend, the reader already knows you, so you can start anywhere. A formal letter goes to someone who has never met you and who is reading it at work, between other jobs. Nothing is shared, so the letter has to carry everything itself.

That is why formal letters look the same everywhere. The layout is a promise to the reader: the sender is at the top, the matter is in the subject line, the details are in the middle, and the request is at the end. The reader does not have to hunt, because every piece is in its usual place. A fixed shape saves the reader's time, and a reader who saves time is more likely to help.

There are three kinds of formal letter you meet in the Class 10 paper:

| Kind | You write to | Your aim |
|---|---|---|
| Letter to the editor | a newspaper's editor, and through them the public and the authorities | to draw attention to a public problem and suggest action |
| Letter of complaint | a manager, officer or company | to get a wrong set right: a refund, a repair, a service restored |
| Order or enquiry letter | a shop, publisher or supplier | to buy goods, or to ask for details such as price and availability |

## What the reader expects {lvl=1 id=s-reader label="Foundation"}

The clerk with the pile asks three questions in a fixed order. Each part of the letter is placed to answer one of them.

| The reader asks | The letter answers with | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| Who is writing, and where do I reply? | the sender's address, date and signature | top and bottom |
| What is this about? | the subject line, and the first sentence of the body | after the receiver's address, before the greeting |
| What do you want me to do? | the request in the last paragraph | the end of the body |

Notice that the middle of the letter, the facts, is not on the list. The facts matter, but the reader only needs them after they know what the letter is for. So the order is purpose first, evidence second, request last.

Two more things a stranger expects. First, **politeness without flattery**: respectful words, but no praise or begging. Second, **brevity**: every sentence earns its place. The board letter is only about 100-120 words, so there is no room for a long welcome or a speech on the state of the world.

## The layout, part by part {lvl=1 id=s-layout label="Foundation"}

A formal letter has the same parts in the same order each time.

| # | Part | What goes there | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sender's address | your address, no name, at the top (right-hand corner in the traditional layout, a neat block at the left is also common) | 15, Gandhi Nagar, Jaipur |
| 2 | Date | day, month in words, year | 14 February 2026 |
| 3 | Receiver's address | the title of the person or office, then the place, below the word To | The Editor, The Daily Chronicle, Jaipur |
| 4 | Subject line | a short phrase that names the matter, no full stop | Subject: Noise from loudspeakers during examinations |
| 5 | Salutation | Sir or Madam, followed by a comma | Sir, |
| 6 | Body | three short paragraphs | see the models below |
| 7 | Subscription | closing phrase matching the salutation | Yours faithfully, |
| 8 | Signature and name | your name below the closing | Sameer Mehta |

:::note title="Faithfully or sincerely?" tone=tip
Match the closing to the greeting. **Sir or Madam** goes with **Yours faithfully**. A named greeting such as **Dear Mr Rao** goes with **Yours sincerely**. The board's letters to an editor, a manager or a supplier almost always start with Sir or Madam, so the closing you need is Yours faithfully.
:::

Some practical rules, based on what examiners look for:

- Use the name and address that the question gives you, or an invented name and address if it tells you to. Never write your own name or roll number in an examination letter. [Unverified: the exact wording of this instruction changes by year, so read it in the paper.]
- Capital letters only for names, the first word of each sentence, and the main words of a title (The Editor, Municipal Corporation).
- Write the subject line as a phrase, not a full sentence: *Subject: Delay in delivery of books ordered on 2 March*.
- Do not begin the body with "I am writing this letter to..." more than once. Say the purpose directly: *I wish to draw your attention to...*

:::qc
q: Which pair of salutation and closing is correct?
options: ["Sir or Madam ... Yours faithfully", "Sir or Madam ... Yours sincerely", "Dear Sir ... Your loving friend", "Respected Sir ... Thanking you, bye"]
answer: 0
right: Right. Sir or Madam takes Yours faithfully. Yours sincerely is for a letter that names the receiver.
wrong: The unnamed greeting Sir or Madam is paired with Yours faithfully. The other closings are for a named person or for an informal letter.
:::

## Formal tone {lvl=1 id=s-tone label="Foundation"}

**Register** is the level of formality in your words. A friend hears "Hey, the shop guy was rude and fixed nothing!" A manager should read: "The salesman refused to replace the item." The facts are the same; the register is different.

| Informal | Formal |
|---|---|
| I'm writing because... | I wish to bring to your notice... |
| Please fix it ASAP! | I request you to resolve the matter at the earliest. |
| It's a total mess. | The situation has become a serious inconvenience. |
| Don't you think...? | I would urge the authorities to... |
| Thanks a lot! | I shall be grateful for your prompt action. |
| It doesn't work. | It has not worked since 4 March. |

Rules of thumb:

1. **No contractions** (do not, I shall, it is) and no slang, no exclamation marks, no emoji or text-speak.
2. **Polite request verbs**: *I request you to*, *I would urge*, *kindly*, *I shall be obliged if*.
3. **Be specific**: dates, amounts, model numbers and road names. A complaint with numbers can be checked, so it is trusted.
4. **Be calm even when angry**. Strong words weaken a complaint. The firmness belongs in the request and the deadline, not in insults.
5. **Use the right tense for the story**: past for what happened (*I bought*), the present perfect for a problem that continues (*has not worked since*), and *shall* or *will* for what you intend (*I shall approach the consumer forum*).

:::qc
q: Which sentence is suitable for a formal letter?
options: ["You guys never listen to us!", "I request you to look into the matter at the earliest.", "It's really bad, so fix it now.", "Hope you'll do something soon!"]
answer: 1
right: Right. It is polite, has no contraction and makes a clear request.
wrong: Look for the sentence with a polite request verb and no slang, contractions or exclamation marks.
:::

## Letter to the editor {lvl=2 id=s-editor label="Class 10"}

A letter to the editor is read by two audiences at once: the editor, who decides whether to print it, and the public and authorities who see it. So it treats a *public* problem (noise, waste, a road, a safety risk) and ends with a *suggestion* for action, not a private demand.

Plan the body in three moves:

1. **Introduce**: say what you wish to draw attention to. The stock opening is *Through the columns of your esteemed daily, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to...*
2. **Describe**: the situation, with one or two concrete details, and who suffers.
3. **Propose**: two or three actions the authorities or the public should take, and a hope that the letter will be published.

:::note title="Model letter to the editor (body: 118 words)"
> 15, Gandhi Nagar\
> Jaipur\
> 14 February 2026
>
> To\
> The Editor\
> The Daily Chronicle\
> Jaipur
>
> Subject: Misuse of loudspeakers during the examination season
>
> Sir,
>
> Through the columns of your esteemed daily, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to the misuse of loudspeakers in our city during the examination season.
>
> Every evening, weddings and processions in our colony play music at full volume until midnight. Students preparing for board examinations cannot concentrate, and many have lost precious hours of study. Patients and elderly residents suffer too, because the noise often crosses the permitted limit.
>
> I urge the police and the municipal corporation to check permissions before every event, enforce the time limit strictly and fine offenders. Organisers, too, should lower the volume until the examinations end.
>
> I hope you will publish this letter so that the concerned authorities act quickly.
>
> Yours faithfully,\
> Sameer Mehta
:::

Why it works: the subject line names the matter; paragraph 1 states the purpose in one sentence; paragraph 2 has a time (every evening, midnight), the people affected and the effect; paragraph 3 gives two clear actions to two clear parties. Even the last line stays polite and does not beg.

:::qc
q: Which line belongs in the last paragraph of a letter to the editor?
options: ["I urge the authorities to enforce the rule strictly and I hope you will publish this letter.", "I am Sameer and I am very angry.", "Please send me a refund of Rs 1,450.", "Dear Editor, love and regards."]
answer: 0
right: Right. The last paragraph proposes action to the authorities and asks for publication.
wrong: The ending of an editor's letter makes a public suggestion. A refund belongs to a complaint, and a personal sign-off to an informal letter.
:::

## Letter of complaint {lvl=2 id=s-complaint label="Class 10"}

A complaint is a request for a remedy dressed in facts. The reader can only help if the story is clear and the remedy is named. Use this plan:

| Paragraph | Job | Typical details |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What you bought or which service, and when | item, model, bill number, date, amount |
| 2 | What went wrong, and what you have already tried | the fault, days since, earlier contact, the reply you got |
| 3 | What you want, by when, and what happens next | replace or refund within seven days; the consumer forum if there is no reply |

The deadline and the next step give the complaint teeth. They are written calmly: *If I receive no reply, I shall be forced to approach the consumer forum.* Mention any papers you enclose.

:::note title="Model complaint (body: 117 words)"
> 22, Lal Kothi Colony\
> Jaipur\
> 12 March 2026
>
> To\
> The Manager\
> Sharma Electricals\
> Station Road, Jaipur
>
> Subject: Defective electric kettle bought on 4 March
>
> Sir,
>
> On 4 March, I bought an electric kettle (model EK-500, bill no. 2187) from your shop for Rs 1,450. Within three days, it stopped heating, and the base now gives off a burning smell.
>
> I took the kettle back on 9 March, but your salesman refused to accept it and said that the shop does not exchange goods once they are sold. This is surprising, as the bill clearly mentions a one-year warranty.
>
> I therefore request you to replace the kettle with a new one or refund the amount within seven days. If I receive no reply, I shall be forced to approach the consumer forum.
>
> I enclose a copy of the bill and the warranty card.
>
> Yours faithfully,\
> Ritu Verma
>
> Enclosures: bill, warranty card
:::

The numbers (dates, model, bill, amount) make the story checkable. The tone is firm because of the deadline, not because of angry adjectives.

:::qc
q: A complaint's last paragraph should do which of these?
options: ["Say what remedy you want and by when", "Repeat the fault in angrier words", "Thank the shop for its excellent service", "Describe your family"]
answer: 0
right: Right. The reader needs to know the action you want and the time you allow.
wrong: A complaint ends by naming the remedy and a deadline. Repeating the fault or praising the shop does not help the reader act.
:::

## Order and enquiry letters {lvl=2 id=s-order label="Class 10"}

These are *business* letters, so exactness matters more than feeling. An order letter that leaves out the quantity or the delivery date will produce a wrong parcel.

### The order letter

An order has a shopping-list job to do. List what you want in a way that cannot be misread, then state the terms.

| Include | Why |
|---|---|
| Item name, catalogue number or title | so the right goods are sent |
| Quantity and the price or rate quoted | so the bill matches your expectation |
| Discount or trade terms | so you are not charged more |
| Packing and mode of dispatch | so goods arrive safe |
| Date and place of delivery | so the order is useful on time |
| Mode of payment | so the supplier knows when and how it will be paid |

:::note title="Model order letter (body: 109 words)"
> Government Senior Secondary School\
> Sector 7, Chandigarh\
> 10 March 2026
>
> To\
> The Manager\
> Learning Hub Books\
> Hazratganj, Lucknow
>
> Subject: Order for books for the school library
>
> Sir,
>
> We are a school library and wish to order the following books from your latest catalogue.
>
> 1. Atlas of the World, 5 copies, at Rs 450 each
> 2. Concise English Dictionary, 10 copies, at Rs 320 each
> 3. Stories of Indian Scientists, 20 copies, at Rs 180 each
>
> Kindly allow us the 15 per cent discount that your catalogue offers on bulk orders. Please pack the books in waterproof cartons and send them by speed post so that they reach us by 25 March. We shall pay by cheque on delivery and on receipt of your bill.
>
> Please confirm the order and the delivery date at an early date.
>
> Yours faithfully,\
> Anil Kumar\
> Librarian
:::

### The enquiry letter

An enquiry asks for information before you buy. State who you are and why you are asking, list the exact questions, and say what you need back and by when.

| Move | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| Who you are, why you ask | *Our school is planning to buy twenty-five laboratory microscopes.* |
| What you want to know | *Kindly send us your price list, the discount for bulk orders, and the warranty terms.* |
| What you need back | *We would also like to know the earliest date by which you can deliver.* |
| Polite close | *An early reply will be appreciated.* |

An enquiry is not an order: it commits you to nothing. Do not write quantities as if you had decided, and do not start paying words such as "we are sending a cheque".

:::qc
q: Which detail is missing from this order? "Please send 20 notebooks at Rs 40 each."
options: ["The date and place of delivery", "The sender's favourite colour", "A joke at the end", "A description of the shop"]
answer: 0
right: Right. Without a delivery date and address, and ideally the payment terms, the supplier cannot act properly.
wrong: An order must say when and where the goods should arrive (and how payment will be made). The other options are irrelevant.
:::

## The board paper and how it is marked {lvl=2 id=s-board label="Class 10"}

In the Class 10 paper, the writing section carries a formal letter of about 100-120 words, worth 5 marks. The question gives you a situation and a short task, for example: *You are Sameer of 15, Gandhi Nagar, Jaipur. Write a letter to the editor of a newspaper about the misuse of loudspeakers.* Sometimes it supplies a few verbal cues or a notice or advertisement to draw the facts from. [Unverified: marks and word count are as remembered from recent papers; read the instructions in your own paper.]

The marks are usually split into **format** (layout and correct parts), **content** (the right ideas, covering the task) and **expression** (grammar, spelling, vocabulary, fluency). A typical split is 1 + 2 + 2. [Unverified: the split varies from year to year; check the current marking scheme.] The checklist below lets you mark your own work.

| Check | Ask yourself | Done? |
|---|---|---|
| Sender and date | Is the sender's address and the date at the top? | |
| Receiver | Is there an office title and place below To? | |
| Subject line | Is there a short phrase naming the matter? | |
| Salutation and closing | Sir or Madam, with Yours faithfully? | |
| Opening paragraph | Is the purpose in the first sentence? | |
| Content | Does the letter use every cue the question gave? | |
| Facts | Are there dates, names, amounts, not just adjectives? | |
| Request | Does the last paragraph say what you want and by when? | |
| Word limit | About 100-120 words in the body? | |
| Tone | Formal, no slang, contractions or exclamation marks? | |
| Accuracy | Spelling, tenses and punctuation checked? | |
| Signature | Is the name from the question (not your own) written at the end? | |

:::worked title="From question to plan in three minutes"
**Question.** Your colony in Bhopal has not had its garbage collected for ten days. Write a complaint to the Municipal Commissioner in 100-120 words. You are Meera Joshi, 8, Shanti Nagar.

**Plan.**
- Subject: Non-collection of garbage in Shanti Nagar since 4 June.
- Paragraph 1: I am a resident of Shanti Nagar; garbage has not been collected for ten days.
- Paragraph 2: Heaps near the park, bad smell, stray animals, risk of disease; two calls to the ward office, no action.
- Paragraph 3: Request daily collection from tomorrow, clearing of the existing heap within three days, otherwise I shall approach the higher authorities.

**Write.** Add the layout, expand each bullet into two or three sentences, check the word count and the checklist. The plan took three minutes; writing it takes ten.
:::

How to use your time: read the question twice, underline the facts, jot the three-paragraph plan, write the letter, then count roughly (about 10-12 words a line in handwriting) and run through the checklist.

:::qc
q: In a 5-mark letter, which of these is least likely to earn marks?
options: ["A subject line naming the problem", "A last paragraph with a clear request", "Your own name and roll number at the end", "Specific dates and amounts"]
answer: 2
right: Right. The question usually gives a name to use. Writing your own name and roll number breaks the exam instructions and can cost marks.
wrong: The other three are all rewarded. Use the name given in the question, never your own.
:::

## Beyond the board: persuading a stranger {lvl=3 id=s-beyond label="Beyond the board"}

Passing the board paper needs the layout and the checklist. Getting something done in life needs more.

### The persuasion ladder

An argument that moves a stranger usually climbs four steps: **fact** (what is happening), **number** (how big it is), **effect** (who is harmed or helped) and **ask** (what exactly to do). Compare "The road is bad" with "Eleven potholes on a 600-metre stretch have caused three accidents this month. I request the corporation to repair it within a fortnight." The second climbs all four steps.

### Firm, not rude

A complaint has two dials: **courtesy** and **pressure**. Keep courtesy high throughout and raise the pressure with facts and next steps, not with adjectives. A polite escalation reads like this: first request, then *I would be grateful for a reply within seven days*, then *I shall be left with no option but to approach...*. Threaten only an action you can really take (the consumer forum, a higher officer, a newspaper).

### Phrase bank

| Job | Useful phrase |
|---|---|
| Open an editor's letter | *Through the columns of your esteemed daily, I wish to draw attention to...* |
| Open a complaint | *I wish to bring to your notice that...* |
| Describe the effect | *This has caused serious inconvenience to...* |
| Request | *I request you to...* / *I shall be obliged if you will...* |
| Add a deadline | *...within a week of receiving this letter.* |
| Close | *I hope the matter will receive your immediate attention.* |

### Email and cover letters

The same ideas carry over to email. The **subject line** matters even more, since it is what is read first. Open with the person's name if you know it (*Dear Ms Rao*) and close with *Yours sincerely*, or *Regards* for routine messages. A **cover letter** for a job or an internship is a formal letter too: it states the post, shows two or three facts that fit it, and asks for an interview. The board does not set it at Class 10, but the three-paragraph plan is the same. [Unverified: the board's Class 10 syllabus lists only enquiry, complaint, order and editor-type letters.]

:::qc
q: Which is the strongest request for a complaint?
options: ["Do something about it.", "Please replace the item or refund Rs 1,450 within seven days.", "I hope things improve.", "You must be ashamed."]
answer: 1
right: Right. It names the remedy, the amount and the deadline, and stays polite.
wrong: A strong request names the action and the time allowed. Vague hopes and insults are weak.

## Key ideas to remember {lvl=1 id=s-keys}

:::keys
1. The reader of a formal letter asks three things in order: who is writing, what is it about, what do you want. {lvl=1}
2. Layout: sender's address, date, To + receiver, subject line, salutation, body, closing phrase, name. {lvl=1}
3. Sir or Madam goes with Yours faithfully; a named greeting goes with Yours sincerely. {lvl=1}
4. Use a formal register: no contractions, slang or exclamation marks; polite request verbs. {lvl=1}
5. Build the body of 100-120 words in three short paragraphs: purpose, facts, request. {lvl=2}
6. The editor's letter treats a public problem and suggests action; a complaint seeks a remedy by a deadline; an order lists item, quantity, price, discount, packing, delivery and payment; an enquiry asks for information. {lvl=2}
7. Use the name and address in the question, not your own, and stay near the word limit. {lvl=2}
8. Marks are usually split between format, content and expression; check against the marking checklist. {lvl=2}
9. Persuade with fact, number, effect and ask; keep courtesy high and let facts supply the pressure. {lvl=3}
:::

## Toolkit and traps {lvl=1 id=s-traps}

:::traps
- No subject line, or one that is a full sentence: write a short phrase naming the matter. {lvl=1}
- Sir or Madam with Yours sincerely, or a named greeting with Yours faithfully: match the closing to the greeting. {lvl=1}
- Contractions, slang and exclamation marks (it's, ASAP, !): keep a formal register. {lvl=1}
- A last paragraph that never says what you want done: end with a clear request. {lvl=2}
- Vague complaints ("the product is bad") with no date, model or amount: give specific facts. {lvl=2}
- Writing your own name or roll number instead of the name given in the question. {lvl=2}
- An order with no delivery date, price or payment terms, or an enquiry that talks as if the order were placed. {lvl=2}
- Copying the question into the letter, or a letter far over or under the word limit. {lvl=2}
- Angry adjectives instead of facts, or threats you cannot carry out. {lvl=3}
:::
