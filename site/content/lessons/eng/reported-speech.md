## Why we need reported speech {lvl=1 id=s-why label="Foundation"}

We cannot always quote. Mostly we pass on what someone said: "Mother said you were late." "The doctor told her to rest." When we do that, we are not recording sound. We are retelling a meaning, and we are doing it at a different moment, in a different place, to a different listener.

That is the first-principles idea of the whole topic: **the words must be re-anchored to the reporter's moment.** A sentence like *I will come here tomorrow* is tied to its speaker, to the moment of speaking and to the place. Move any of the three and the sentence stops making sense.

| Anchor | Question it answers | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The speaker and listener | who? | pronouns (and possessives) |
| The time of speaking | when? | tense, and words like today or tomorrow |
| The place of speaking | where? | here, this, come |

Remember the three words: **who, when, where.** The rest of this lesson is the machinery for changing them correctly.

**Direct speech** repeats the exact words, in quotation marks:

*Asha said, "I am hungry."*

**Indirect (reported) speech** gives the meaning from the reporter's side, with no quotation marks:

*Asha said that she was hungry.*

The part outside the quotation marks (*Asha said*) is the **reporting clause**. What was said is the **reported clause**.

## Statements: the three changes {lvl=1 id=s-statements label="Foundation"}

### 1. Person: who

Pronouns follow the reporter's position, not the speaker's.

| Direct word | Becomes | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| I, we, my, our (first person) | the subject of the reporting clause | *Gita said, "I am late."* becomes *Gita said that she was late.* |
| you, your (second person) | the object of the reporting clause | *Gita said to me, "You are late."* becomes *Gita told me that I was late.* |
| he, she, they (third person) | no change | *Gita said, "He is late."* becomes *Gita said that he was late.* |

The easy memory aid is **"1-2-3: subject, object, no change."** The first person goes to the subject, the second person goes to the object, the third person stays. Always ask who is who in the retelling, because the aid fails if the reporter was himself the listener (as in the hook, where *you* became *me*).

### 2. Tense: when

The reporting verb is usually in the past (*said*). The reported action then happened even earlier than the saying, or was in the future from that point. So the verb steps one tense back.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| present simple: *I like tea.* | past simple: *she said she liked tea.* |
| present continuous: *I am reading.* | past continuous: *she said she was reading.* |
| present perfect: *I have finished.* | past perfect: *she said she had finished.* |
| past simple: *I saw him.* | past perfect: *she said she had seen him.* |
| will: *I will go.* | would: *she said she would go.* |
| can: *I can swim.* | could: *she said she could swim.* |
| may: *I may come.* | might: *she said she might come.* |
| shall: *We shall win.* | would: *he said they would win.* |

Some words do not move: *would, could, should, might, ought to, must* (for obligation), and the past perfect stay as they are. A past simple may stay unchanged when the sentence already has a clear time marker, but the safe board answer is to step it back.

### 3. Time and place: when and where

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the week before / the previous week |
| next month | the following month |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this, these | that, those |
| come | go |

*Ravi said, "I will meet you here tomorrow."* becomes *Ravi said that he would meet me there the next day.*

Note that these changes are made because the reporter is standing somewhere else, at another time. If the reporter is in the same place on the same day, *here* and *today* may reasonably stay. The table is the default for the board.

### The joining word

For statements, *that* joins the clauses and may be dropped in speech: *She said that she was tired* or *She said she was tired.* After *said to*, change to *told* in most answers: *She said to me, "I am tired"* becomes *She told me that she was tired.*

:::qc
q: Mohan said to Seema, "I shall help you tomorrow." Choose the correct report.
options: ["Mohan told Seema that he would help her the next day.", "Mohan told Seema that I should help you tomorrow.", "Mohan told Seema that he will help her tomorrow.", "Mohan told Seema that she would help him the next day."]
answer: 0
right: Right. I goes to the subject, you to the object, shall to would, and tomorrow to the next day.
wrong: Do the three changes in order. The first person is Mohan, the second is Seema. Then step shall to would and tomorrow to the next day.
:::

## Questions, commands and exclamations {lvl=2 id=s-types label="Class 10"}

A question, a command and an exclamation each have a feature that a plain statement does not. The reported form must handle that feature.

### Questions

A direct question has an inverted word order (*Where do you live?*) and a question mark. A reported question is no longer a question to the listener. It is part of a statement, so it takes **statement word order, no helper do/does/did, and a full stop**.

**Yes/no questions** use *if* or *whether*:

- *She asked, "Are you ready?"* becomes *She asked if I was ready.*
- *He said to me, "Do you play chess?"* becomes *He asked me whether I played chess.*

**Wh-questions** keep the question word and drop the helper:

- *She asked, "Where do you live?"* becomes *She asked where I lived.*
- *The teacher asked, "Why were you absent?"* becomes *The teacher asked why I had been absent.*
- *He asked, "What is your name?"* becomes *He asked what my name was.*

The reporting verb changes from *said* to *asked*, *enquired* or *wanted to know*. Never write *asked me that*.

### Commands and requests

A command has no subject in the direct form. In the reported form it becomes a to-infinitive with an object.

- *The teacher said to us, "Sit down."* becomes *The teacher told us to sit down.*
- *Mother said to him, "Please wash your hands."* becomes *Mother requested him to wash his hands.*
- *The officer said to the guard, "Don't open the gate."* becomes *The officer ordered the guard not to open the gate.*

Choose the verb from the tone: *ordered, commanded, told* (strong); *requested, asked, begged* (polite); *advised, warned, urged* (guidance); *forbade* (negative prohibition, used as *forbade him to go*). A negative command takes *not to*, never *to not*.

### Exclamations and wishes

An exclamation expresses feeling. Report the feeling with a verb such as *exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise* or *cried out*, then use *that*, and turn the exclamation into a statement. Drop the exclamation mark.

- *Arjun said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."* becomes *Arjun exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.*
- *He said, "Alas! I am ruined."* becomes *He exclaimed with sorrow that he was ruined.*
- *She said, "What a lovely view!"* becomes *She exclaimed with delight that it was a very lovely view.* (or: *exclaimed that the view was lovely*)

Wishes use *wished* or *prayed*: *"May you live long!"* becomes *She wished that I might live long.* The board usually asks the common exclamation type, not the rare wish type.

### Greetings, thanks and sentences with let

- *"Good morning, sir," said the boy.* becomes *The boy wished the teacher good morning.*
- *"Thank you, Aunt," said Rani.* becomes *Rani thanked her aunt.*
- *"Let's go out," said Kabir.* becomes *Kabir suggested that they should go out.* (Not "suggested to go").

:::qc
q: "What are you reading?" the librarian asked Dev. Choose the correct report.
options: ["The librarian asked Dev what he was reading.", "The librarian asked Dev what was he reading.", "The librarian asked Dev that what he was reading.", "The librarian asked Dev what you were reading."]
answer: 0
right: Right. Statement word order, no helper, and the pronoun you changes to he.
wrong: A reported question follows statement order, so was he reading is wrong. Also check the pronoun: Dev is the listener, so you becomes he.
:::

## Reporting verbs and the full conversion routine {lvl=2 id=s-verbs label="Class 10"}

Said is a plain verb. Better answers use a verb that carries the speaker's attitude, because the reporter's aim is to retell the meaning.

| Sentence in the original | Useful reporting verb | Pattern after it |
|---|---|---|
| agrees | agreed, admitted | that + clause |
| refuses to do | refused | to + base verb |
| says sorry | apologised | for + -ing, or that + clause |
| promises | promised | to + base verb, or that + clause |
| denies | denied | -ing, or that + clause |
| suggests | suggested | that + subject + should / -ing |
| advises | advised | object + to + base verb |
| warns | warned | object + (not) to + base verb |
| reminds | reminded | object + to + base verb |
| accuses | accused | object + of + -ing |
| congratulates | congratulated | object + on + -ing |

Examples:

- *"I'm sorry I broke your vase," said Isha.* becomes *Isha apologised for breaking my vase.*
- *"I didn't take your pen," Leela said to me.* becomes *Leela denied taking my pen.*
- *"Don't forget to lock the door," Dad said to me.* becomes *Dad reminded me to lock the door.*

Use these verbs only when the original clearly shows the attitude. If it does not, *said* or *told* is safe. A verb that changes the meaning is a mistake, not a style choice.

### The conversion routine

Work in the same order each time. This is the checklist from the margin:

1. **Find the reporting verb and the type.** Statement, question, command or exclamation? Pick the verb and the joining word.
2. **Fix the pronouns** with 1-2-3: subject, object, no change.
3. **Fix the tense** by stepping one back.
4. **Fix time and place words.**
5. **Fix the order and punctuation.** Remove quotation marks, remove the question mark or exclamation mark, use statement word order.
6. **Read the final sentence aloud** and check that it sounds like something a person would say.

### Several sentences in one speech

A speech of two or three sentences is reported as a single paragraph. The sentences are joined with *and, that,* or a new reporting verb for each sentence.

*The doctor said to the patient, "You are very weak. Take rest for a week and do not go out. Will you follow my advice?"*

*The doctor told the patient that he was very weak. He advised him to take rest for a week and not to go out, and asked whether he would follow his advice.*

Notice that each sentence type gets its own reporting verb. Do not keep repeating *said that*.

## When not to backshift {lvl=2 id=s-exceptions label="Class 10"}

The tense change is only needed when the reporting verb is in the past. These cases keep the original tense:

| Case | Example |
|---|---|
| Reporting verb in the present or future | *She says, "I am tired."* becomes *She says that she is tired.* / *He will say, "I am busy"* becomes *He will say that he is busy.* |
| A universal truth or a scientific fact | *The teacher said, "The Moon moves round the Earth."* becomes *The teacher said that the Moon moves round the Earth.* |
| A habit or fact still true at the time of reporting | *He said, "I live in Delhi."* becomes *He said he lives in Delhi* (if he still does). |
| Past perfect in the direct speech | *She said, "I had left."* becomes *She said she had left.* |
| Modals that are already past or have no past form (would, could, should, might, ought, must) | *He said, "I must go."* becomes *He said he must go* / *had to go.* |

The universal-truth rule is the one the board tests most often. *Said that the Earth moved round the Sun* is the typical wrong answer.

Remember that the exception about "still true" is a judgement, so a mark scheme may prefer the backshifted form unless the sentence is a general truth. When in doubt, follow the rule given in your own textbook. [Unverified] as to how strictly the board's marking schemes apply the habit rule.

## Direct from indirect, and common board tasks {lvl=2 id=s-board label="Class 10"}

The board also asks you to go the other way, from indirect to direct.

*He said that he was going to the market that evening.* becomes *He said, "I am going to the market this evening."*

Work backwards. Remove *that*, put quotation marks round the words, step the verb forward one tense, and restore *this, here, now* and so on. Put a comma before the opening quotation mark and a capital letter at its start.

### Error-correction questions

A typical sentence-with-error question contains one of these faults:

| Fault | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| *said me* | He said me that he was ill. | He told me that he was ill. |
| helper left in a question | She asked me where was the station. | She asked me where the station was. |
| no *if/whether* | She asked me did I know him. | She asked me if I knew him. |
| *to not* | He told me to not run. | He told me not to run. |
| forgot to change time words | He said that he would come the next day tomorrow. | He said that he would come the next day. |
| pronoun not changed | She said that I am late. | She said that she was late. |
| wrong verb for the type | He said me to sit. | He told me to sit. |

### Tips for the exam

- Read the question once as a whole before changing a word.
- Write the reporting verb first. Then every other change follows.
- Do not forget that after *told* and *asked* an object is needed: *told me, asked him*. *Said* takes *to* before the object: *said to me*, which becomes *told me*.
- Check the full stop or question mark at the end.

## Beyond the board: viewpoint and finer points {lvl=3 id=s-beyond label="Beyond the board"}

### Backshift is viewpoint, not a law

A reporter who treats a statement as still true may keep the present: *She said she lives in Pune.* The backshifted *lived* is not wrong, but it makes it unclear whether she still does. Careful writers use this to avoid confusion. Textbooks differ, and the board's safe answer is to backshift unless the statement is a general truth. [Unverified]

### Reporting without quoting: free indirect speech

In fiction, authors sometimes merge the narrator's voice with a character's thoughts: *She looked at the sky. Would it rain? Surely it would.* There is no reporting clause. The tense and pronouns follow the narrator, but the word order and emotion follow the character. You will meet this style when reading the literature chapters. It is not set in the board grammar paper.

### Modals and the reported subjunctive

After verbs such as *suggested, insisted, demanded* and *proposed*, many writers use *should* or the bare form: *He suggested that she (should) apply.* The pattern is a reported instruction, not a statement.

### Reporting what a text says

Present tense reports what a written text says: *The poem says that ...*, *The author argues that ...*. This is not backshift. It is the literary present, and it is the form used in the board's analytical writing.

## Key ideas to remember {lvl=1 id=s-keys}

:::keys
1. Reported speech retells the meaning of someone's words from the reporter's viewpoint. Three anchors move: who (pronouns), when (tense and time words) and where (place words). {lvl=1}
2. Pronouns follow 1-2-3: first person to the subject, second person to the object, third person unchanged. {lvl=1}
3. After a past reporting verb the tense steps one back: am/is to was, will to would, can to could, present perfect and past simple to past perfect. {lvl=1}
4. Time and place words change: now to then, today to that day, tomorrow to the next day, yesterday to the day before, here to there, this to that. {lvl=1}
5. Statements take that; yes/no questions take if or whether; wh-questions keep the wh-word. Reported questions use statement order with no helper. {lvl=2}
6. Commands and requests become to + base verb (negative: not to) after told, ordered, asked, requested, advised. {lvl=2}
7. Exclamations become statements with exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise that; greetings and thanks use wished and thanked. {lvl=2}
8. Choose a reporting verb that shows the speaker's attitude (apologised, denied, promised, suggested, reminded), only when the meaning supports it. {lvl=2}
9. Do not backshift when the reporting verb is present or future, or when the statement is a universal truth. {lvl=2}
10. Beyond the board: backshift reflects viewpoint, reports of written texts use the present, and fiction uses free indirect speech. {lvl=3}
:::

## Toolkit and traps {lvl=1 id=s-traps}

:::traps
- "He said me that he was ill": use told with an object, or said (to me) that. {lvl=1}
- Keeping the quotation marks or the question mark in indirect speech. {lvl=1}
- Leaving the pronouns unchanged: "She said that I am late" when she meant herself. {lvl=1}
- Forgetting the time words: tomorrow must become the next day. {lvl=1}
- "She asked where was the station": drop the inversion, write where the station was. {lvl=2}
- "He asked me did I know him": add if or whether and use statement order. {lvl=2}
- "He told me to not run": write not to run. {lvl=2}
- Applying 1-2-3 mechanically when the reporter was the listener: check who is who. {lvl=2}
- Backshifting a universal truth: "said that the Earth moved round the Sun". {lvl=2}
- Repeating said in a long speech instead of choosing a verb for each sentence type. {lvl=2}
:::
