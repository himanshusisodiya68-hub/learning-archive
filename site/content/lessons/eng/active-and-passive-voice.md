wrong: Look at the subject of each sentence. In the first and third, the doer (the thief, someone) is the subject. Only in "The window was broken" is the thing acted on the subject, and that is the passive.

## Why English has two voices {lvl=1 id=s-why label="Foundation"}

Every event has at least two parts: someone or something that **does** the action, and someone or something that **receives** it. Look at one event:

- A dog bit the postman.

You can start the sentence from either end. Start from the doer and you get the **active voice**. Start from the receiver and you get the **passive voice**.

- Active: *The dog bit the postman.*
- Passive: *The postman was bitten by the dog.*

The facts are the same. What changes is the **focus**: which thing is placed first, where the reader's attention lands. Ask yourself, "What is this sentence about?" In the active sentence it is about the dog. In the passive it is about the postman.

This is why the language needs both. Sometimes the receiver is the star of the story (*The postman was bitten*), sometimes you do not know who did it (*My phone was stolen*), and sometimes the doer is too obvious to mention (*The thief was arrested*, obviously by the police). A language with only one voice would force you to invent a doer in every sentence.

:::note title="Voice is about the subject" tone=tip
Voice answers one question: **is the subject doing the action or receiving it?** Subject does it: active. Subject receives it: passive. Do not confuse voice with tense; every tense has both voices.
:::

## Active voice: the default {lvl=1 id=s-active label="Foundation"}

In an active sentence the order is: **subject (doer) + verb + object (receiver)**.

| Subject (doer) | Verb | Object (receiver) |
|---|---|---|
| Meena | painted | the wall. |
| The cook | is making | soup. |
| Farmers | grow | wheat. |

Most of our sentences are active because it is the natural order of thought: who did what. It is short and direct, which is why it is the default in stories, speeches and everyday talk.

## Only transitive verbs have a passive {lvl=1 id=s-trans label="Foundation"}

To make a passive, the object of the active sentence must become the new subject. So the sentence must **have** an object. A verb that takes an object is **transitive**; a verb that does not is **intransitive**.

| Verb type | Test | Example | Passive? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transitive | You can ask "what?" or "whom?" after the verb | *Ravi kicked the ball.* (kicked what? the ball) | Yes: *The ball was kicked by Ravi.* |
| Intransitive | Nothing to ask; the verb is complete | *The baby slept.* *The train arrived.* | No |

So *The sun rises* cannot become a passive, and *He was slept* is not English. Some verbs can be either: *She sang* (intransitive) and *She sang a song* (transitive). Only the second can become *A song was sung by her*.

:::qc
q: Which sentence cannot be changed into the passive voice?
options: ["The tourists admired the fort.", "The tourists arrived early.", "The tourists visited the museum."]
answer: 1
right: Right. Arrived is intransitive, with no object, so there is nothing to turn into a new subject.
wrong: Ask "what?" or "whom?" after the verb. You can ask "admired what?" and "visited what?" but not "arrived what?". Only the transitive verbs have a passive.
:::

## Building the passive: the formula {lvl=1 id=s-form label="Foundation"}

The passive has three pieces: the **new subject** (the old object), a form of **be**, and the **past participle (V3)** of the main verb. The old subject, if kept, comes at the end after **by**.

> **Object + be (in the same tense) + V3 + by + subject**

Take a simple example, step by step: *Neha writes a letter.*

1. Find the object: *a letter*. It becomes the new subject.
2. Find the tense of the verb: *writes* is present simple.
3. Use the present simple of **be** that agrees with the new subject: *is*.
4. Put the main verb in V3: *written*.
5. Add the old subject with **by**: *by Neha*.

Result: **A letter is written by Neha.**

Note: after the change, **be** carries the tense and the number (is/are, was/were), while the main verb is always V3. Many learners put the tense on the wrong word.

### Pronouns change form

When the doer or receiver is a pronoun, the form changes with its job. Subject forms (I, he, she, we, they) become object forms (me, him, her, us, them) after *by*, and the other way round.

- *She helps me.* becomes *I am helped by her.*
- *They invited us.* becomes *We were invited by them.*

## The passive in every tense {lvl=1 id=s-tenses label="Foundation"}

The helper **be** takes the tense of the original verb. Here is the whole set for a transitive verb, using *write* as the example.

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | Neha writes a letter. | A letter is written by Neha. |
| Past simple | Neha wrote a letter. | A letter was written by Neha. |
| Future simple | Neha will write a letter. | A letter will be written by Neha. |
| Present continuous | Neha is writing a letter. | A letter is being written by Neha. |
| Past continuous | Neha was writing a letter. | A letter was being written by Neha. |
| Present perfect | Neha has written a letter. | A letter has been written by Neha. |
| Past perfect | Neha had written a letter. | A letter had been written by Neha. |
| Future perfect | Neha will have written a letter. | A letter will have been written by Neha. |

Patterns that save memory:

- **Continuous** adds **being**: is/are/was/were + **being** + V3.
- **Perfect** adds **been**: has/have/had/will have + **been** + V3.
- **Future** adds **be**: will + **be** + V3.
- The simple present and simple past use only is/are or was/were.

Plural subjects take *are/were/have*: *Letters are written*, *Two letters were written*, *Letters have been written*.

The **perfect continuous** tenses (has been writing, had been writing, will have been writing) have a passive too, but it is clumsy (*has been being written*) and is almost never used. The board does not ask for it, so a sentence in these tenses is normally rewritten or left active. [Unverified] as to whether any board has ever set one.

:::qc
q: Choose the passive form of "The workers are repairing the road."
options: ["The road is repaired by the workers.", "The road is being repaired by the workers.", "The road has been repaired by the workers.", "The road was being repaired by the workers."]
answer: 1
right: Right. The active verb is present continuous, so the passive uses is being + V3.
wrong: The active verb is "are repairing", present continuous. Its passive adds being: is/are + being + V3, and the new subject is singular (the road), so is being repaired.
:::

:::qc
q: Choose the passive form of "The school has announced the results."
options: ["The results were announced by the school.", "The results have been announced by the school.", "The results are announced by the school.", "The results had been announced by the school."]
answer: 1
right: Right. The active verb is present perfect (has announced), so the passive is have been + V3, and the new subject results is plural.
wrong: The active verb has + V3 is present perfect. Perfect goes to has/have been + V3, and results is plural, so have been announced.
:::

## When to use the passive {lvl=1 id=s-when label="Foundation"}

Do not change a sentence to the passive just because you can. Use it for a reason.

1. **The doer is unknown.** *My bicycle was stolen.* (We do not know who.)
2. **The doer is obvious.** *The injured were taken to hospital.* (By the ambulance crew, obviously.)
3. **The doer is unimportant.** *Rice is grown in wet fields.* (Who grows it is not the point.)
4. **The action or the receiver matters more.** *The vaccine was developed in two years.* *The Taj Mahal was built in the seventeenth century.*
5. **You do not want to name the doer.** *Mistakes were made.* (Often used to avoid blame; be careful of this in your own writing.)
6. **A formal, impersonal tone.** Notices and science reports: *Visitors are requested to keep silence.* *The mixture was heated for ten minutes.*

In these cases the **by-agent is dropped**: *My bicycle was stolen*, not *My bicycle was stolen by someone*. If the doer matters, keep it: *The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci.*

### When to prefer the active

If the doer is known and important, the active voice is shorter and clearer. Compare:

- Passive: *The ball was kicked by Ravi into the net.*
- Active: *Ravi kicked the ball into the net.*

The active sentence says the same thing with fewer words. A long chain of passives can make writing dull and hide who is responsible, so the active is the better default for stories and personal letters.

## Common mistakes at the foundation level {lvl=1 id=s-mist label="Foundation"}

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *The letter is wrote by Neha.* | *The letter is written by Neha.* | The main verb must be V3. |
| *The letter written by Neha.* | *The letter is written by Neha.* | A form of *be* is missing. |
| *The window broken yesterday.* | *The window was broken yesterday.* | *Be* must carry the tense. |
| *The match was win by us.* | *The match was won by us.* | V3 of win is won, not win. |
| *The cake is ate by the children.* | *The cake is eaten by the children.* | V3 of eat is eaten, not ate. |

Learn the V3 forms of common irregular verbs: write/written, take/taken, give/given, break/broken, eat/eaten, see/seen, know/known, speak/spoken, do/done, choose/chosen.

## Changing voice: the board's method {lvl=2 id=s-method label="Class 10"}

In the board paper, a "change the voice" question gives you sentences and expects you to convert them. Follow the same steps each time.

1. **Mark the three parts:** subject, verb (with its tense), object.
2. **Swap places:** the object moves to the front, the subject moves to the end after *by*.
3. **Rebuild the verb:** be (right tense, agreeing with the new subject) + V3.
4. **Fix the pronouns:** *I/he/she/we/they* become *me/him/her/us/them* when they move to the *by* phrase, and the other way round.
5. **Check:** read the sentence once. Tense unchanged? V3 used? Agreement right?

:::worked title="Four conversions in a row"
(a) *The teacher praised the students.* Object: the students. Tense: past simple, so *were* (plural). Passive: **The students were praised by the teacher.**

(b) *They are building a new bridge.* Object: a new bridge. Tense: present continuous, so *is being*. Passive: **A new bridge is being built by them.**

(c) *Someone has stolen my watch.* Object: my watch. Tense: present perfect, so *has been*. "Someone" is vague, so drop the agent. Passive: **My watch has been stolen.**

(d) *We will finish the work by Friday.* Object: the work. Tense: future simple, so *will be*. Passive: **The work will be finished by us by Friday.** [Many keys prefer **The work will be finished by Friday** because "by us" is obvious.]
:::

## Questions in the passive {lvl=2 id=s-quest label="Class 10"}

Questions follow the same idea, but the word order of the question must be kept straight. Do not panic; turn the question into a statement first if it helps.

### Yes/no questions

Keep the helper at the front, move the new subject after it.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Does Rina cook dinner? | Is dinner cooked by Rina? |
| Did he break the glass? | Was the glass broken by him? |
| Have you finished the exercise? | Has the exercise been finished by you? |
| Will they invite her? | Will she be invited by them? |

### Wh- questions

- If the **wh- word is the object** (who, whom, what): it stays at the front. *What did she cook?* becomes *What was cooked by her?* *Whom did they invite?* becomes *Who was invited by them?*
- If the **wh- word is the subject**: *Who broke the glass?* becomes *By whom was the glass broken?* The old subject (who) moves to the end and becomes *by whom*, which comes first in the question.

*Who wrote this poem?* becomes *By whom was this poem written?* *What* as a subject (*What caused the delay?*) is normally left in the active because the passive sounds odd. [Unverified]

## Modals, infinitives and gerunds {lvl=2 id=s-modal label="Class 10"}

### Modal verbs

A modal (can, could, may, might, must, should, would, shall, will) is followed by **be + V3** in the passive. The modal itself does not change.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| You must finish the task. | The task must be finished. |
| We can solve this problem. | This problem can be solved. |
| They should follow the rules. | The rules should be followed. |
| She may have lost the keys. | The keys may have been lost. |

The last row shows the perfect modal: *modal + have + been + V3*.

### Infinitives

*To + base verb* becomes *to be + V3* in the passive.

- *She hopes that they will select her.* can be written *She hopes to be selected.*
- *Someone needs to clean the room.* becomes *The room needs to be cleaned.*
- *He wants to see the manager* has no natural passive; leave it active.

### Gerunds

*Doing* becomes *being done*: *I remember them telling me.* becomes *I remember being told by them.* *He dislikes people laughing at him.* becomes *He dislikes being laughed at.*

## Two objects and prepositional verbs {lvl=2 id=s-two label="Class 10"}

### Verbs with two objects

Some verbs (give, send, tell, show, teach, offer, lend) have a **direct object** (the thing) and an **indirect object** (the person who receives it). Either can become the subject of the passive.

- Active: *The principal gave Anil a medal.*
- Passive 1 (indirect object first, the usual choice): **Anil was given a medal by the principal.**
- Passive 2 (direct object first): **A medal was given to Anil by the principal.**

Both are correct. Passive 1 is more common in speech, and boards usually accept both.

### Verbs with prepositions

A verb plus its preposition stays together, and the preposition is left behind at the end.

- *The children laughed at the clown.* becomes *The clown was laughed at by the children.*
- *We looked after the garden.* becomes *The garden was looked after by us.*
- *She turned down the offer.* becomes *The offer was turned down by her.*

Do not drop the preposition or split it from the verb: *The clown was laughed by the children* is wrong.

## Commands, requests and special sentences {lvl=2 id=s-cmd label="Class 10"}

### Commands (imperatives)

A command has no stated subject (you is understood). The passive is made with **Let + object + be + V3**.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Close the door. | Let the door be closed. |
| Do not touch the wires. | Let the wires not be touched. (or: The wires must not be touched.) |
| Please shut the window. | You are requested to shut the window. |
| Bring me a glass of water. | Let a glass of water be brought to me. |

Polite requests commonly turn into *You are requested to...* or *You are advised to...* in the passive.

### Sentences with "it" and "there"

Verbs of saying and thinking (say, think, believe, report, expect, know) often form an impersonal passive.

- *People say that honesty pays.* becomes **It is said that honesty pays.**
- *They believe that the old house is haunted.* becomes **It is believed that the old house is haunted.** or **The old house is believed to be haunted.**

### Keeping the agent: when "by" is not the right word

*By* usually introduces the doer. Some passives use another preposition and they are fixed by habit, so learn them:

- *The room was filled with smoke.* (not by)
- *The ground was covered with snow.*
- *I was surprised at the news.* / *He is interested in music.* / *She is known for her honesty.* / *The mixture is made of sand and cement.*

Here the participle works almost like an adjective. They are not strictly "agent" passives, but the board's fill-in-the-blank prepositions often test them.

:::qc
q: Choose the correct passive of "Please bring the register."
options: ["The register is brought.", "You are requested to bring the register.", "The register was brought by you.", "Bring the register by you."]
answer: 1
right: Right. A polite request becomes "You are requested to..." in the passive. "Let the register be brought" is also acceptable.
wrong: The sentence is a request, and a request keeps its force in the passive through "You are requested to..." or "Let ... be ...". It must not become a statement in the past or present.
:::

:::qc
q: "The clown was laughed ___ by the children." What fills the gap?
options: ["at", "to", "of", "no word is needed"]
answer: 0
right: Right. The active verb is laugh at, and the preposition stays with the verb in the passive.
wrong: The phrasal verb is "laugh at". In the passive the preposition remains after the participle: was laughed at.
:::

## Passive in the board's questions {lvl=2 id=s-board label="Class 10"}

Boards usually test voice in one or more of these ways. [Unverified] as to the exact format in any particular year; check a recent sample paper.

1. **Change the voice** of 3 to 5 sentences. Marks go for the right tense of *be*, the right V3, and correct agreement.
2. **Fill the gap with the passive form** of the verb in brackets (often in a passage or a notice).
3. **Error correction** where the error is a missing *be*, a wrong V3, or an active sentence left in the middle of a passive passage.
4. **Reporting a notice or an experiment** in the passive (*A circuit was set up. The current was measured...*). This is not a transformation but a writing style.

### Quick checklist

- Did I keep the **same tense** as the original?
- Is the main verb in **V3**?
- Does **be** agree with the **new subject** (is/are, was/were, has/have)?
- Did I handle the **pronouns** correctly?
- Did I drop the agent only when it is vague (someone, people, they, one)?

:::worked title="From a lab notice to a passive report"
Active notes: *We heated the water. We added salt. We stirred the mixture and noted the temperature.*

Passive report: **The water was heated. Salt was added. The mixture was stirred and the temperature was noted.**

The doer ("we") is dropped because the experiment, not the experimenter, is what the reader needs. Notice that every sentence keeps the past tense.
:::

:::qc
q: Find the error: "The exercise has been solve by all the students."
options: ["has been should be have been", "solve should be solved", "by should be with"]
answer: 1
right: Right. After has been the main verb must be V3 (solved).
wrong: The passive needs be + V3, and "solve" is the base form, not the past participle. The helper "has been" already matches the singular "exercise".
:::

## Beyond the board: choosing a voice {lvl=3 id=s-beyond label="Beyond the board"}

Voice is not only a grammar rule to apply; it is a **writing tool** to choose.

### Topic and flow

A sentence reads smoothly when old, known information comes first and new information comes last. The passive lets you keep the same topic as the subject across two sentences.

- Weaker: *The committee chose Priya. Everyone admired her work.*
- Smoother: *Priya was chosen by the committee. Her work was admired by everyone.* (both sentences stay about Priya)

Also use the passive when the doer is long and the receiver short: *The bill was passed by the members of the committee after three days of debate* reads better than putting that long phrase first. [Inference] about general style; not a board rule.

### Passive in science and law

Science writing uses the passive to put the experiment in the foreground: *Samples were collected from three sites.* Many journals today allow the active as well. Legal and official documents use it for formality and for rules that apply to anyone: *Smoking is prohibited in this area.* Here the doer is "anyone", so naming it adds nothing.

### Hiding the doer

The passive can hide who is responsible. Compare *The company dumped waste in the river* with *Waste was dumped in the river*. The second sentence is true yet leaves the guilty party unnamed. A careful reader asks of any passive sentence: **who did this, and why is it not said?** A careful writer uses the passive for clarity, not to avoid blame.

### The "get" passive

In speech and informal writing, *get* sometimes stands in for be: *He got caught. She got promoted. My bag got stolen.* It often suggests something unplanned or unlucky. Use *be* in formal answers.

### Stative passives

*The shop is closed.* can mean "someone is closing it" (action) or "it is shut at the moment" (state). The second reading is a state, and the participle acts like an adjective: *closed*, *broken*, *tired*, *married*. Compare *The shop is closed at five* (state or habit) with *The shop was closed by the owner at five* (action). Context decides. [Inference]

### Transitive verbs with no passive

A few verbs have no passive even though they take an object: *have* (in the sense of possess), *resemble*, *fit*, *suit*, *lack*. We say *She has a car* but not *A car is had by her*; *He resembles his father* but not *His father is resembled by him*. This is a lexical property, and exceptions are learned through reading. ## Key ideas to remember {lvl=1 id=s-keys}

:::keys
1. Voice tells you whether the subject does the action (active) or receives it (passive). The facts stay the same; the focus changes. {lvl=1}
2. Only transitive verbs (those with an object) can be made passive. {lvl=1}
3. Formula: new subject (old object) + be in the same tense + V3 [+ by + old subject]. {lvl=1}
4. Continuous gets being (is being done); perfect gets been (has been done); future gets be (will be done). The main verb is always V3. {lvl=1}
5. Use the passive when the doer is unknown, obvious, unimportant or less important than the action; drop the by-agent in those cases. {lvl=1}
6. Questions keep their helper in front: Is dinner cooked by Rina? A subject who becomes By whom was the glass broken? {lvl=2}
7. Modals take be + V3: The task must be finished. Perfect modals take have been + V3. {lvl=2}
8. Two-object verbs have two passives: Anil was given a medal. A medal was given to Anil. {lvl=2}
9. Commands become Let + object + be + V3, or You are requested to... for polite requests. {lvl=2}
10. Beyond the board: the passive helps topic flow and formal tone, but can also hide the doer; choose the voice for a purpose. {lvl=3}
:::

## Toolkit and traps {lvl=1 id=s-traps}

:::traps
- Using V1 or V2 after be: "is write", "was wrote". The main verb must be V3. {lvl=1}
- Leaving out be: "The letter written by Neha." {lvl=1}
- Making an intransitive verb passive: "He was slept", "The train was arrived". {lvl=1}
- Changing the tense in the passive: the tense of be must match the tense of the active verb. {lvl=1}
- Wrong V3 of irregular verbs: "eaten" not "ate", "broken" not "broke", "spoken" not "spoke". {lvl=1}
- Forgetting being with a continuous tense: "The road is repaired" does not mean "is being repaired". {lvl=2}
- Wrong agreement of be with the new subject: "The results was announced". {lvl=2}
- Keeping a vague agent: "by someone", "by people" are normally dropped. {lvl=2}
- Dropping the preposition of a verb phrase: "The clown was laughed by..." should be "laughed at". {lvl=2}
- Not changing pronoun forms: "by he" should be "by him". {lvl=2}
- Choosing the passive when the doer is important and known, which makes the sentence long and weak. {lvl=3}
- Using the passive to hide responsibility in an answer or a letter of complaint. {lvl=3}
:::
